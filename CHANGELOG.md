# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file. The project
had no formal releases or tags before this changelog was written, so the
versions below were reconstructed from the git history and applied
retroactively. Versioning follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/):
`MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`, with the project still in its `0.x` initial-development
phase (breaking changes can happen in a minor bump).

## [0.59.0] - 2026-08-05
- The scraper reads seventeen venues directly now, not just metalgigs and petzi. Those two aggregators cover the big rooms — Kiff, Dynamo, Sedel, Kofmehl, Z7 — and systematically miss the spaces this site exists for: of the venues with the most events, the ones with no aggregator link at all were almost entirely squats, autonomous centres and small bars, every show typed in by hand. The new sources are the other end of that list: Die Cafete, KUZEB, Post Squat, Alte Post, Provitreff, QuaiDuBas30, Rümpeltum, Bad Bonn, Kaschemme, Werkk, Horstklub, Gare de Lion, TapTab, Treppenhaus, Eldorado, Nouveau Monde and Safari Bar.
- Sources are declared rather than hardcoded. `_scrape_and_import` used to build its work list from two literal comprehensions naming metalgigs and petzi inline, which does not survive going to nineteen; there is now a registry in `diytracker/services/venue_sources.py` holding one entry per venue — its key, the venue identity, the fetcher, the contact address, the language of its site — and the scrape loop iterates that. Adding a venue is one entry plus one function in `venue_parsers.py`. Each entry also carries a note saying *why* it reads what it reads, because "there is a feed but we don't use it" is the kind of decision that otherwise survives only in a commit message.
- Where a venue publishes something machine-readable, that is what gets read: Horstklub's `events.csv` (the same file its own front page loads), Treppenhaus's and Eldorado's REST endpoints, TapTab's Joomla RSS, and the JSON Provitreff's Next.js build embeds in its homepage. The rest are listing pages with hooks worth trusting — Bad Bonn hangs title, date, time and price off each anchor; Werkk and QuaiDuBas30 use `<time datetime>`. Two venues, Gare de Lion and Safari Bar, hide the date from their listing and need a second request per event, so they keep the old discover-then-parse shape and the URL dedup that goes with it. Rümpeltum and Safari Bar are frankly fragile and are marked as such in the code.
- Reading a feed is not the same as reading it correctly, and two bugs here would have been invisible. Alte Post and Post Squat both advertise an iCal export, which looked like the obvious source until it turned out to return the *oldest* fifty events and ignore both `scope` and `limit` — a stale archive containing none of the current programme, which a parser would have imported without complaint. Both venues are read from their listing pages instead. Separately, the iCal helper converts UTC to Swiss wall-clock time, because Alte Post publishes `Z` timestamps and `Event.date` holds the hour a show starts as entered: importing 19:00 UTC verbatim would have put every summer show two hours early.
- Two traps particular to venue scraping are handled rather than discovered later. `ScrapedEvent.url` is UNIQUE, so a venue that lists ten shows on one page with no per-event links cannot put that page's URL on all ten rows — nine would silently vanish as duplicates; those rows carry a `source_id` digest instead and dedup through the `(source, source_id)` constraint. And venue name, PLZ and canton come from the registry, copied verbatim from the rows already in the database including their existing trailing spaces, rather than from whatever the site prints — otherwise ingest quietly creates a second "Alte Post" next to the real one.
- Eight of the twenty-five venues considered deliberately have no scraper, and the reasons are recorded in code so the next person does not spend an afternoon rediscovering them. Photobastei's `robots.txt` disallows the entire site, which the scraper already obeys, so a parser would be dead code; Picadilly posts its programme as flyer images with no text; Moshpit's programme page is empty and lives on Facebook; Grüntal, Bahnhöfli and Toms Beer Box have no website at all; Château d'Erguël has no venue site; and "Röonda" could not be identified as a venue at all and needs its name checked against whoever entered it.
- `docs/venue_permission_emails.md` holds a drafted mail to each venue asking whether reading their public event pages is okay, in German or French to match the venue's own site, with an explicit opt-out. Everything the drafts claim about the scraper's manners is true of the code — honest User-Agent, robots.txt respected, delays between requests, immediate stop on a 403. Three sends need a judgement call and say so: Alte Post publishes no plain-text address (the one in the file is decoded from their deliberate anti-harvesting obfuscation), Horstklub publishes only a merch-orders inbox despite having the most convenient feed of the lot, and Photobastei's mail has to acknowledge that their robots.txt already said no.
- **Server-side:** no migration and no new dependency — iCal, RSS and CSV are parsed on the stdlib and bs4. A normal git pull, `uv sync` and restart. Worth watching the first scrape in the log: each venue reports its own count, and a source returning zero is logged as a warning, since a broken parser and a venue with nothing booked look identical in the queue.

## [0.58.0] - 2026-07-29
- The site explains itself now. A promoter who logged in landed on "YOUR LABELS" with no hint of what a label is here, why shows they never entered were sitting in their dashboard, what claiming does, or where the view counts come from — all of it was knowledge you had to be told in person. The dashboard gained a collapsible "How this page works" block covering exactly those five things; it starts open for a promoter with no labels yet and collapsed for everyone else, so the tour appears the one time it is worth reading.
- A public `/help` page carries the long version, split into sections with stable anchors — account and roles, adding a show, creating and managing labels, the public label page, claiming shows, the numbers and notifications, and genres. Each backstage page it documents (dashboard, label form, claim page, submit form, genres) links straight to its own section, and "Help" joins the backstage nav bar. The page sits in the sitemap and `llms.txt` alongside `/about`, since "how do I get my shows listed" is a question people arrive with rather than one they only ask after logging in.
- The help text follows the legal documents' pattern rather than the translation catalogs: one template per locale under `templates/help/`, German authored first and FR/IT/EN translated from it. Long prose in a `.po` file is unreviewable and every edit churns four catalogs; only the short UI strings — the nav item, the link labels, the dashboard block — went through Babel.
- **Server-side:** nothing beyond the usual pull and restart — no migration, no new configuration.

## [0.57.1] - 2026-07-29
- `/set-language/` stopped being a crawl trap. A client identifying as `apache-httpd` was walking the site hard and concentrating on that one route, which turned out to be the site's own fault: the language switcher puts a `/set-language/<lang>?next=<path>` link on *every* page, one per locale, and each of those redirects to a page carrying the same four links again — so a crawler following links multiplied its work instead of converging, and none of it was cacheable, because the route writes a session and every response therefore carries a `Set-Cookie`. The switcher links are now `rel="nofollow"`, `robots.txt` disallows `/set-language/`, and the redirect itself answers with `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow` for crawlers that never read robots.txt. Nothing changes for a person clicking the switcher.
- The route also has a limit now — 30 requests an hour per IP, on the reasoning that a real visitor switches language a handful of times and anything past that is not reading the site. Alongside it the whole app gained a default ceiling of 120 requests a minute per IP, which no human browsing comes near but a crawler walking every filter combination does. Both are in-memory and per-worker, so with four gunicorn workers the effective ceiling is up to four times that; logged-in users and static files are exempt.
- New `diytracker/services/abuse.py` rejects clients that have no business browsing the site at all — currently the Apache HttpClient family — with a plain-text `429` and a `Retry-After`, before any template render or database query. It is deliberately separate from the scrape detector, which scores traffic and never blocks: this one does no scoring and keeps no state, just a substring match on the User-Agent, because the agents on the list are HTTP libraries rather than browsers with an odd string and nobody loses a page they wanted. `/api/ingest` is exempt, since it is token-authenticated and *meant* to be called by scripts. Two new optional variables, `BLOCKED_USER_AGENTS` and `BLOCKED_IPS`, extend the built-in list from `.env`, so the next abusive client can be shut out with a restart instead of a deploy.
- **Server-side:** no migration and no nginx change — a normal git pull, `uv sync` and restart. Afterwards `sudo bash deploy/traffic_audit.sh` with `DAYS=1` is the check that the `/set-language/` share has actually dropped.

## [0.57.0] - 2026-07-26
- Every list screen in the admin TUI can be filtered. Events dumped 200 rows, users, labels and the audit log dumped everything, and finding one row meant scrolling — so `/` now opens a filter prompt on all five (an empty query clears it, and the subtitle spells out an active filter, because a filtered table looks exactly like a short one). The matching is the site search's own dialect, reused rather than reinvented: terms are ANDed, `%` and `_` are escaped so a query of "%" filters instead of matching everything. Events search name, line-up, genre, venue, city and submitter; labels also match their slug and their owner's address; the audit log's `/` search combines with the existing `f` action filter rather than replacing it.
- Venues are no longer reachable only through dedup. A new **Venues** screen lists every venue with its city, canton, PLZ, event count and whether it has accessibility answers, and edits all six address fields in one dialog — the everyday case of a wrong city or a missing PLZ, which until now meant opening the database by hand. The canton goes through the same `resolve_canton()` every other write path uses, so typing "Zürich" stores `ZH` rather than a value the canton pages can't filter on. Deleting refuses while events still point at the venue (that case wants a merge in Venue dedup, not an orphaning), and takes the accessibility record with it when it does delete. The screen shows *whether* a venue has an accessibility link, never the link: that token overwrites a venue's answers with no history, so it stays out of terminal scrollback exactly as it stays off public pages.
- New **Ops** screen for the two things that otherwise need a second terminal: purging the page cache (`instance/cache`, the manual step after any template change) and looking after the scraper. It shows when the last scrape ran, when the next one is due, what the queue looks like by status, and the URLs recently skipped — and can trigger a run. The trigger is confirm-gated and says plainly what it cannot know: the running-lock is per process, so the admin tool cannot see a scrape already running inside a gunicorn worker. Overlapping runs re-fetch the same pages but cannot create duplicate events, since ingest still dedups on URL.
- Traffic auditing answers three questions it couldn't before. **Scrapers** narrows the picture to the offenders — crawler, bot and suspicious IPs — and adds what makes a decision possible: how much of the window each one is, its peak requests per minute, how many distinct days it came back on, which traps it hit, and whether the live detector agrees. **Hosts** groups traffic by where it resolves to, using both a reverse-DNS registrable domain (so "these fourteen bot IPs are all AWS" becomes visible) and the /24, because either alone lies: a PTR record is missing for plenty of hosts, and a subnet says nothing about who owns it. **Devices** breaks the human share down by form factor, OS and browser, parsed from the UA with the same regex approach as the rest of the module rather than a new dependency — and states its own caveat, that the unit is an IP over the window and not a person.
- PTR lookups are cached in a new `ip_hostname` table, misses included: a lookup is a network round trip, so resolving inline would stall the screen and re-resolving on every refresh would re-ask the same questions all day. A refresh resolves at most 200 addresses, busiest first; anything over that budget shows as pending and fills in next time, so the view never blocks on DNS.
- The traffic window is capped at 90 days. "All logs" grew with every rotated file on disk and mixed year-old traffic into a picture of what the site is doing now; the timeframes are 7, 30 and 90 days, and the 90-day key reuses the date range the analytics module already had. Under the hood the log parsing moved into one shared scan, so the four log-driven views cost one pass each instead of one pass per statistic.
- Also fixed along the way: a crashing manual scrape could leave the process permanently marked as "scraping" and the trigger dead until restart; the Labels screen could raise on a keypress that arrived before its first refresh finished; and every doc still told you to run `manage.py`, which was renamed to `db_admin_cli.py` some releases ago. The TUI now has real tests — Textual's Pilot mounts all fourteen screens and every traffic view against the test database, which the previous import-only smoke test could never have caught.
- **Server-side:** no migration script this time — `ip_hostname` is a new table, so `db.create_all()` makes it on start. Note that the Hosts view does outbound PTR lookups from the server the first time it runs.

## [0.56.0] - 2026-07-26
- The promoter dashboard shows a per-event trend, not just totals. Each event row gains a 30-day sparkline (inline SVG, no chart library) of daily page views, fed by the same `EventDailyViews` pipeline as the cumulative counts — so a promoter can see whether a shared link actually moved the needle, not just the all-time sum.
- A new **"In review — likely yours"** panel on the dashboard lists pending scrape-queue entries whose title, line-up or organizer fuzzily matches one of the promoter's label names (same matcher as the claim page's suggestions). It's read-only by design — the queue stays admin-only — but it means a promoter learns their show is already in the pipeline instead of submitting it a second time. A panel was chosen over a digest email: no opt-in machinery, no SMTP failure modes, and promoters visit the dashboard anyway.
- Promoters get an email when an admin edits an event carrying their label — until now such edits were silent and the promoter found out when the public page looked different. The mail is plain-text German (like the invite emails, deliberately outside the Babel catalogs), links the event and the dashboard, and is sent only when the editor isn't the label's owner. It's opt-out via a toggle on the dashboard (`submitter.notify_label_events`, default on — the userbase is small and invited). Sending happens after the commit and never breaks the edit: an SMTP failure is logged to `logs/notifications.log` and swallowed. Queue approvals don't trigger it, since approvals never attach a label.
- **Server-side:** run `uv run python scripts/migrate_add_notify_flag.py` once before restarting (adds the opt-out column, default on; idempotent), then restart and delete the script. SMTP uses the existing `EMAIL_*` variables — nothing new to configure.

## [0.55.0] - 2026-07-26
- The scrape queue has a real rejection state. "Remove from queue" used to set the same `approved` flag as publishing, so a rejected entry was indistinguishable from a published one except by its missing event link, and there was never a reason on record. `scraped_event` now carries an authoritative `status` — `pending` / `published` / `rejected` — plus an optional `reject_reason`; every read in the app (web queue, TUI duplicate review, dashboard stats, konzibot dedup candidate scan, the queue-duplicates script) now goes through `status`. The legacy `approved` boolean is still written alongside for this one release as a safety net for any stray script, then becomes write-only history. Rejecting from the web asks for an optional reason (a prompt on the per-row button, a shared field on bulk reject) and both the column and the audit-log entry carry it.
- Queue moderation got the bulk of its missing ergonomics: a search box over title, line-up, venue, city and organizer (same term-ANDing and wildcard escaping as the site search), pagination at 50 rows, and bulk rejection via row checkboxes with one shared reason. There is deliberately **no bulk approve**: approving runs venue-resolution and event-creation on raw scraped strings with default door times, and waving several through unreviewed is exactly how near-duplicate venues get minted — single approve stays per-row.
- Duplicate triage no longer requires the terminal. Flagged possible-duplicates (hidden from the main queue since 0.51.0) are now also reviewable at `/queue/duplicates`, a small admin page that reuses the TUI's discard/unflag logic from `diytracker/admin/queue_review.py` verbatim — same rules, same audit entries, wherever the click happens. The main queue links to it whenever something is flagged.
- **Server-side:** run `uv run python scripts/migrate_add_queue_status.py` once before restarting — it adds `status`/`reject_reason` and backfills existing rows (approved with an event → `published`; approved without → `rejected`, reason left empty since historic rejections never had one). Idempotent; delete it afterwards. Note that historic "removed from queue" rows will show as rejected with no reason.

## [0.54.0] - 2026-07-26
- Labels have a public profile beyond name, logo and plain-text description. Four new optional fields on the label form — website, two social links, and a public contact email — render in the label page's hero as a link row (external links carry `rel="nofollow noopener"`, the email as `mailto:`). URLs go through the same `SafeLink` scheme validation as ticket links, and the template re-checks `is_safe_link()` before rendering, so a `javascript:` value can neither be saved through the form nor rendered if one ever reached the database another way.
- The label description is linkified on the public page: a new `linkify` Jinja filter escapes the whole text first and then wraps only plain `http(s)` URLs in anchors, so a promoter can finally write "alle Infos auf unserer Seite: …" with a working link while everything else stays inert text. Same XSS posture as 0.45.0 — unsafe schemes are never anchored.
- Logos can be removed, not just replaced. The edit form shows a "Remove the current logo" checkbox when a logo exists; a new upload in the same submit wins over the checkbox. The file stays on disk (matching the replace path, which never deleted old files either) — only the reference is cleared.
- Under the hood, flyer/logo upload handling now guards on `FileStorage`: a form built with `obj=...` leaks the *stored path string* into the file field's data when a POST carries no file part at all (browsers always send an empty part, so this never fired in practice — but a scripted client omitting the input would have crashed the edit views with a 500). The forms also switched to Flask-WTF's Werkzeug-aware `FileField`.
- **Server-side:** run `uv run python scripts/migrate_add_label_profile.py` once before restarting (adds the four `label` columns; idempotent), then restart and clear the page cache (`rm -rf instance/cache/*` — label pages are cached per-locale). Delete the script afterwards.

## [0.53.0] - 2026-07-26
- The promoter claim page is no longer a flat dump of every unlabelled event. It was a single unpaginated list of all upcoming events without a label, so finding your own five shows meant scrolling everything the scrapers had brought in. The page now has a search box (`?q=`, same term-ANDing and wildcard escaping as the site search, matching event name, acts, venue and city) and pagination at 50 rows. On top sits a **"Likely yours"** block: the user's label names are fuzzy-compared against event names and line-ups — with the same diacritics-folding normalisation the konzibot dedup uses, so "Kälte" still matches "Kaelte" — and matching events are offered with the right label preselected. Suggestions are computed against the full unfiltered list, so a likely match can't be hidden behind a search term or on page three.
- Claimed events can be released again. Claiming was one-way: a slip of the dropdown was permanent until an admin intervened in the database. Every event row on the promoter dashboard now has a *Release* button (with confirmation) that detaches the event from the label and leaves it on the calendar. Strictly per-account like claiming itself: only the label's owner can release, admins get no special path. Claims and releases both land in the audit log from 0.52.0.

## [0.52.0] - 2026-07-26
- Moderation and promoter actions are now recorded in an audit trail. Until now nothing kept a record of who approved a queue entry, deleted an event, or claimed an event for a label — a wrong approval or a disputed claim could only be reconstructed by memory. A new `action_log` table (created automatically by `db.create_all()`, so no migration script) stores each action with a UTC timestamp, the actor (the account's email, snapshotted so the row stays readable if the account is later deleted; `tui` for the admin tool), the target, and a free-text detail line. Logged actions: queue approve/reject/unflag (web and TUI), admin event edits and deletes, and label create/edit/delete/claim from both the promoter area and the TUI's Labels screen. The writer (`services/audit.py`) stages the row on the caller's transaction, so the log entry and the change it describes commit or roll back together.
- The approve log entry also fixes a quiet attribution loss: approving a queue entry stamps the approving admin as the event's submitter and always discarded the scraped record's own attribution. The event keeps the admin as submitter (that drives permissions and "added by"), but the log detail now preserves the source, URL, organizer and source-side submitter of what was actually approved, linked to the created event id.
- The admin TUI gained an **Audit log** screen: newest-first table of all entries, `f` filters by action name or prefix (e.g. `label.`), and selecting a row shows its full detail. Read logic lives in `diytracker/admin/audit.py`, shared-layer style.

## [0.51.1] - 2026-07-26
- The submit form no longer offers other promoters' labels. `/submit`'s label dropdown was populated with every label on the site, so any logged-in account could publish an event under any promoter's label — the event then landed on that label's public page and its promoter dashboard with nobody warned, the exact misattribution the 0.49.0 promoter-area fix was about, still open on the submission path. The dropdown is now restricted to labels the submitting account owns, admins included (consistent with 0.49.0's decision to drop admin special-casing: the silently preselected first option is precisely how misclaims happen). Ownership is enforced server-side through the field's choices, not just hidden in the UI, so a forged `label_id` in the POST is rejected too. Admins can still attach any label where that is deliberate moderation: the admin edit-event form keeps the full list, as does the TUI's Labels screen. The helper split lives in `services/labels.py` (`owned_label_choices()` next to the now admin-only `all_label_choices()`), and the claim page's own-labels query moved there as shared `owned_labels()`.

## [0.51.0] - 2026-07-24
- Possible-duplicate queue entries are now kept out of the web approval queue entirely and reviewed in their own admin-TUI screen. Since 0.48.0 the stricter konzibot dedup flagged same-night collisions with `needs_review` and floated them to the top of `/queue` behind a "⚠ Possible duplicate" banner — but they still sat in the moderation list, one warning among the genuinely new events, easy to approve on autopilot and so defeating the point of flagging them. The web queue now filters out every `needs_review` row (`parse_scraped_events` in `blueprints/submissions.py`), so it shows only clean entries. The flagged ones move to a new **Queue duplicates** screen in the admin TUI (`uv run python manage.py`), which lists each one with the calendar/queue entries it collided with and offers two actions per row: *Discard* (it really is a duplicate — drops it from the queue without publishing, the same as the web delete) or *Unflag* (a false positive — clears the flag so it returns to the web queue for the normal approve/edit flow). The review logic lives in `diytracker/admin/queue_review.py` as plain functions, shared-layer style.
- A one-shot `scripts/scan_queue_duplicates.py` backfills the flag across the existing queue. The ingest-time check only runs on konzibot pushes, so duplicates that were already staged before 0.48.0 — or that arrived through eventbot/petzi/metalgigs — sit unflagged. The script re-runs the same same-day collision detection (`services/ingest_dedup.py`, now able to exclude a row from matching itself) across the whole staged queue regardless of source and sets `needs_review`/`review_reason` on the offenders, pulling them into the new review screen. It only looks at current-or-future events (a duplicate of a show that already happened isn't worth reviewing), and takes `--dry-run` to preview without writing, `--source` to limit to one source, and `--no-date-cap` to scan every future row rather than the default today..+2-months window; it is idempotent and safe to re-run.
- **Server-side:** no schema change (the `needs_review`/`review_reason` columns landed in 0.48.0). After deploying, run `uv run python scripts/scan_queue_duplicates.py` once on the server to flag the existing backlog, then restart as usual.

## [0.50.1] - 2026-07-23
- Festivals now appear on every day they run. A multi-day event has always carried an `end_date` — the `.ics` feed and the schema.org data both use it — but the calendar grouped events by their start date only, so a three-day festival showed up on its opening day and then vanished from the rest of its own weekend. Worse, once the opening day had passed, the "upcoming events" filters on the canton, genre, label and venue pages dropped the event entirely, so a festival disappeared from those pages while it was still going on; on the main calendar the same thing happened when the start date fell before the three-month query window. Grouping now expands an event across its whole date range, so a festival gets an entry under each of its days, and everywhere that filtered on "start date is in the future" now also keeps events whose `end_date` hasn't passed. Past days are still cut off as before — an ongoing festival shows its remaining days, not the ones already over.

## [0.50.0] - 2026-07-23
- The admin TUI has a Labels screen. Until now labels could only be touched through the promoter web area, which is strictly per-account — so once the admin special-casing was removed in 0.49.0 there was deliberately no place left to moderate labels across accounts, and anything cross-account (fixing a typo in someone else's label name, moving a label to a different owner, cleaning up an abandoned one) meant opening the database by hand. The new screen lists every label with its slug, owner and event count, and can create, rename, edit the description, reassign to another promoter and delete. Creating and reassigning only offer accounts that actually have the promoter flag, since a label owned by a non-promoter would be invisible to its own owner. Renaming regenerates the slug the same way the web form does and warns that the old public `/label/<slug>/` URL stops working; deleting detaches the label's events first (they stay on the calendar, just unlabelled), mirroring the promoter-side delete. The logic lives in `diytracker/admin/labels.py` as plain functions, shared-layer style, so a scripted CLI surface can reuse it later if one is ever wanted.

## [0.49.0] - 2026-07-23
- Admins no longer share one promoter view. The promoter dashboard, the claim page's label dropdown, `label_stats()` and the label edit/delete ownership check all special-cased admins to see and act on *every* label, and that mixed badly with the claim form: its `<select>` has no placeholder, so the browser silently preselects the first option — which for an admin was whatever label sorts first alphabetically, not their own. In practice every account on the site is an admin, so when one promoter claimed their five shows the form defaulted to the other promoter's "DIYTracker" label and all five landed on the wrong public label page and the wrong dashboard, with nobody warned. The promoter area is now strictly per-account: everyone, admin or not, sees only the labels they own, can only claim events for those, and can only edit or delete their own. Admin-wide label moderation, if it's ever needed, belongs in the admin tool rather than in a shared web view that makes misattribution the default.
- **Server-side:** the five misclaimed events need to move to their real label in the production database. After deploying, run `uv run python scripts/reassign_diytracker_label_events.py` once on the server (it prints what it moves, clears the page cache, and is a no-op on re-run), then delete the script.

## [0.48.0] - 2026-07-23
- Events pushed by konzibot now get a much stricter duplicate check before they reach the queue. The unified ingest only ever deduplicated on a canonical URL or `(source, source_id)`, so when konzibot re-pushed a show that was already on the calendar or already sitting in the queue — same night, same city, but under a fresh id — it sailed straight through as a brand-new entry and an admin had to notice the collision by eye. For `source=konzibot` only, ingest now runs an aggressive same-day match against both the live calendar and the unapproved queue: it normalises hard (folds diacritics, strips a leading PLZ and a trailing canton, collapses whitespace) and fuzzy-compares city, venue and title, so "kiff"/"KIFF" and "aarau"/"Aarau" still line up and a differing title doesn't hide the match. Sharing the date plus any one of those signals is enough. A hit doesn't drop the event — it is still staged, but flagged `needs_review` with a short summary of what it collided with, surfaces at the top of `/queue` behind a "⚠ Possible duplicate" banner listing the calendar and queue entries it matched, and is approved or deleted with the usual buttons. Other sources (eventbot, petzi, metalgigs) are untouched.
- The same konzibot path also stops trusting konzibot's spelling of things. It routinely ignores the API contract — cantons as full names instead of ISO codes, genres and venue names that don't match what the site already has — so before staging, a konzibot payload now has its genres snapped onto the catalog's canonical casing (`hardcore` → `Hardcore`) and its venue name matched to an existing venue's exact spelling when one exists in the same city, so approval reuses that venue instead of quietly minting a near-duplicate. Canton full names already resolved to codes for every source via `resolve_canton`; that is unchanged.
- **Server-side:** this adds two columns to `scraped_event` (`needs_review`, `review_reason`). Since the schema comes from `db.create_all()` and there is no Alembic, run `uv run python scripts/migrate_add_needs_review.py` on the server once after deploying (it is idempotent), then restart. Existing rows default to not-flagged.

## [0.47.1] - 2026-07-22
- The shared event-cards partial no longer walks the calendar. It rendered by looping over *every day* of every month it spanned and asking whether that date had any events — roughly ninety iterations to produce the thirty-odd cards the calendar actually shows. Feeding that loop was the only reason `months_data` existed, which in turn was the only reason `_months_data()`, `_months_spanning()` and a `datetime=datetime` context variable existed, threaded through five view functions. Since `grouped_events` is already a date-keyed mapping, the partial now iterates it directly, the way `archive_month.html` and the search page's past-events branch always had; the supporting machinery is gone. Behaviour is unchanged — same cards, same order, same today-onward cutoff — but a day with no shows can no longer produce an empty card, and a test now pins that.
- Canton, genre and label pages had their `<title>` and `<meta name="description">` written out by hand in the template and then written out a second time in `public.py` to feed the page's JSON-LD. The two copies had already drifted: a label page told search engines "Upcoming shows by X — dates, venues, prices and accessibility info." in its meta tag and "Upcoming shows by X." in its structured data, so the same page described itself two different ways depending on which one a crawler read. Both now come from a single `page_title`/`page_description` pair built in the view, and a test asserts the meta tag and the JSON-LD stay byte-identical. The hero headings stay in the templates, since those are deliberately different copy.
- The admin event list measured "upcoming" against the wrong clock. `Event.date` holds the local Swiss wall-clock time a show starts, but `admin/events.py` filtered it against UTC, so during CEST a concert stayed in the admin's upcoming list — and in the dedup scan — for two hours after it had already dropped off the public calendar, which filters the same column against local time. The event cards had the mirror-image bug: they took "today" from `datetime.utcnow()`, so between midnight and 02:00 Swiss time the site still considered it yesterday and stamped yesterday's card with the TODAY marker. Both now use the local clock. The underlying cause was that the rule was never written down — `utcnow()`'s own docstring claimed *all* comparisons must use it, which is true of the audit columns it was written for and wrong for the wall-clock ones; it now says which columns it means, and `CLAUDE.md` records the distinction.

## [0.47.0] - 2026-07-22
- The search box moved out of the public nav and into the calendar's filter modal, where it sits above the tag filters. In the nav it was a second input under the links on every single page, competing with them for the one band of dark space under the banner and pushing the actual content further down; searching and filtering are the same intent anyway, so the one place people already open to narrow the listings is where the field belongs. It is still a plain GET form that works without JavaScript and still leaves the query in the URL, and `/search` keeps its own prominent, autofocused field, so a result page can be refined without going back to the calendar. The trade-off is that the modal only exists on the calendar, so pages like an event or venue page no longer carry an entry point to search.

## [0.46.0] - 2026-07-22
- Events can now be put into a calendar app, which was previously impossible — the site published dates and left it to the reader to retype them into their phone. Every event page has an "Add to calendar" button serving a single `.ics` file, and `/calendar.ics` is a subscribable feed of everything coming up in the next six months. The feed takes the same filters the landing pages use (`?canton=`, `?genre=`, `?label=`), so the canton, genre and label pages each offer a "Subscribe to these shows" block with a `webcal://` link, a plain download and a copy button: one URL that keeps updating on its own is the closest thing the site has to "follow this scene", and it is shareable as-is. An unknown slug on the feed 404s rather than quietly returning everything, so a subscription someone else passed on can't silently widen. Serialisation is hand-written in `diytracker/services/ics.py` (no new dependency) and derives start and end times exactly the way the page's schema.org data does, so the two can never disagree; times carry an embedded `Europe/Zurich` VTIMEZONE rather than floating, which would land at the wrong hour for anyone whose device is not on Swiss time. UIDs are stable per event and `SEQUENCE` follows `updated_at`, so editing a listing updates the entry in an existing subscription instead of duplicating it.
- The site has a search box. There was no way to look anything up: if you knew the band's name, your only options were the canton/genre/label pages or scrolling the calendar, whose filter modal is client-side and leaves no trace in the URL. `/search?q=` matches across event name, line-up, description, genre, venue name and city; multiple words are ANDed, so "punk bern" narrows instead of returning both sets, and a query of `%` or `_` matches nothing rather than everything (the wildcards are escaped). Results render as the same event cards as everywhere else, with a toggle for past events that switches to the archive's dated-list layout, and the field sits in the public nav on every page as a plain GET form that works without JavaScript. The results page is `noindex` and `/search` is disallowed in `robots.txt`, since an unbounded query space has nothing to offer an index; it is also deliberately uncached, because caching single-use queries would just fill the on-disk page cache. Matching is plain SQL `LIKE` rather than SQLite FTS5: without Alembic an FTS virtual table can't come from `db.create_all()`, and at this corpus size it isn't needed — the trade is written down in `diytracker/services/search.py` for whoever revisits it.
- `scripts/i18n_populate.py` now compiles each catalog to its `.mo`, which it had never done despite the workflow in `CLAUDE.md` saying it did. Nothing else compiled them either — `i18n_status.py` reads the `.po` files and CI does the same — so a catalog could report 100% coverage while the running site served the previous release's strings, because Babel only ever reads the `.mo`. This was caught while verifying the strings added above: they were fully translated in all four languages and still rendered in English in the browser.

## [0.45.0] - 2026-07-22
- Ticket links can no longer smuggle script into the page. `ticket_link` had no scheme validation anywhere, and while the event page checked for `http://`/`https://` before rendering its href, the shared event cards — the ones on the calendar, and on every canton, genre and label page — did not. A stored `javascript:` link therefore ran in the visitor's browser the moment they clicked "Tickets" on the site's busiest page, and it could be planted by any invited user via `/submit`, by anyone approving a queue entry through the `override_ticket_url` field, or by any holder of `INGEST_TOKEN` over `POST /api/ingest`. The scheme is now checked in all three places: a `SafeLink` validator on `EventForm`, `clean_ticket_url()` on the ingest path, and a shared `is_safe_link()` Jinja global guarding both templates, so a bad value is rejected at the door and would still not render if one were already in the database. Existing rows are unaffected — nothing is rewritten, the link is simply not shown if it isn't `http(s)`.
- The app now sets security response headers on everything it serves, which it previously did not do at all: `Strict-Transport-Security` (only over TLS, so a plain-http dev server doesn't pin localhost for a year), `X-Content-Type-Options`, `X-Frame-Options`, `Referrer-Policy` and `Permissions-Policy`. They live in the app (`diytracker/services/security.py`) rather than in nginx so they ship with a normal git deploy and can be tested; the nginx reference config gained only `server_tokens off` and a `nosniff` on `/static/`, which nginx serves without ever reaching gunicorn. A Content-Security-Policy ships alongside them but as **`Report-Only`** for now: it is nonce-based on purpose (`script-src 'unsafe-inline'` would re-permit exactly the `javascript:` hrefs above), and enforcing it today would break the calendar, because a dozen templates still use inline `onclick=`/`onsubmit=` handlers and because the public page cache stores rendered HTML before the header is added, so a cached page's nonce no longer matches the one in its own response. Both blockers are written up in the module docstring, and a test pins the Report-Only mode so switching it on has to be a deliberate change.
- The scrape queue is admin-only. `/queue` and `/queue/<id>/delete` were guarded by `login_required`, which since there is no public signup was being read as "is trusted staff" — but it only means "holds an account". Every invited user could therefore approve queue entries onto the live calendar and permanently delete the queue, with no undo and no record of who did it, which is a much bigger grant than an invite is meant to be and could not be revoked short of deleting the account. Both routes now require `admin_required`; `/submit` and the genre list stay at `login_required`, since submitting events is precisely the capability an invite exists to hand out. The backstage nav was already role-gated, so the Queue link simply moved from the shared links to the admin ones and no longer renders for users who would get a 403 from it.
- **fail2ban was not running on the production box, and the diytracker jail had never been installed at all.** The service had been dead since 2026-07-07 — `jail.d/` held only Debian's stock sshd stub, and `filter.d/diytracker-login.conf` did not exist, so the install steps in `deploy/` had never been run. The cause is that the host is journal-only with no `/var/log/auth.log`, while the stock `[sshd]` jail resolves its logpath there through `paths-common.conf`; with `backend = auto` fail2ban 1.0.2 aborts on the missing file, and one unopenable log in one enabled jail takes the whole service down with it, silently. The reference jail config had the wrong premise written into it as a comment ("auth.log exists on Debian 12 so the stock filter/backend work as-is") and now sets `backend = systemd` instead, `python3-systemd` being installed. Two further fixes there: `dbpurgeage` is raised to 8d, because Debian's 1d default discards the ban history long before `bantime.maxtime = 1w` can be reached and so `bantime.increment` never actually escalated; and an `ignoreip` placeholder was added so enabling the jail can't lock you out of your own admin login. Nothing suggests the gap was exploited — SSH is key-only with root login disabled, so the failed attempts in the journal could not have succeeded, and the app log shows five failed logins in total. Search-engine crawlers cannot trip the login jail: its filter matches only the app's "Failed login attempt" warning, which only a failed `POST /login` writes. **Server-side:** the filter and jail must be installed by hand and the result verified with `fail2ban-client status`, not just a restart — `deploy/README.md` now spells this out, since `enabled = true` in a config file says nothing about whether the service actually came up.
- The contact form on `/about` is rate-limited to 5 posts per hour per IP. Each submission opens an authenticated SMTP session and sends a real email, and the only thing standing in front of it was a honeypot field — anything that filled the form correctly could flood the inbox unmetered and get the sending account throttled by the provider.
- Logging in no longer carries the pre-login session over into the authenticated one. `session["user_id"]` was assigned onto whatever session the visitor already had, at both `/login` and `/set-password/<token>`; both now clear it first, keeping only the chosen language, which is a preference rather than a credential.
- `deploy/logrotate-diytracker` is a new reference config for the app's own logs, which nothing was rotating: `logs/` had reached 336 MB, with the 95 MB error log being re-scanned by the fail2ban jail on every pass. **Server-side:** it has to be installed by hand once (`sudo cp deploy/logrotate-diytracker /etc/logrotate.d/diytracker`), like the other files in `deploy/`; see `deploy/README.md`.

## [0.44.0] - 2026-07-21
- SQLite now enforces foreign keys (`PRAGMA foreign_keys=ON` on every connection, alongside the existing WAL/busy-timeout pragmas). It never did before, which is how production ended up with nine events pointing at venues that had been deleted out from under them — every hit on those event pages (e.g. `/events/283/`) crashed with a 500 — plus 129 scrape-queue rows whose approval link pointed at since-deleted events. The delete paths that relied on unenforced FKs were fixed to detach first: deleting an event (admin form and TUI) now nulls any `scraped_event.approved_event_id` pointing at it, deleting a venue removes its accessibility row along with it, and deleting a user who still owns labels is refused with a clear message instead of leaving labels pointing at a ghost promoter (label deletion already detached its events). Covered by a new `tests/test_fk_enforcement.py`. **Server-side:** the orphaned rows themselves were repaired directly in the production database (four deleted venues recreated under their original ids from the scraper's own venue data, the other events repointed to the venues the scraper had already recreated, dangling approval links nulled) — the enforcement pragma would otherwise make the existing bad rows fail on update. Deploy needs the usual git-pull + restart; no schema change.
- The admin TUI gained a Traffic screen (`manage.py` → Traffic) that analyses the access logs already on disk — nginx's in production, gunicorn's own in dev — instead of adding any client-side tracking. Its Users view scores every client IP on behaviour a combined log line can actually witness (exploit-path probes, the `/events/archive/` honeypot, scripted user agents, UA rotation, bursts, metronomic request timing, event-id enumeration, sessions that never touch the home page, all-error histories) and buckets them into human / crawler / bot / suspicious / unclear, with the fired signals spelled out and a per-day requests / unique IPs / estimated-humans trend on top. Selecting an IP shows its full profile, including whether the live scrape detector has independently flagged it. The Events view ranks events by their recorded daily view counts with a per-day sparkline, so traffic distribution over time is visible per event. Weights and the bot threshold are calibrated against the existing scrape detector and `deploy/traffic_audit.sh`, so all three tools tell the same story. Logic lives in `diytracker/admin/traffic.py` with tests; nothing needs to change on the server, since the deploy user can already read `/var/log/nginx/access.log`.
- The Traffic screen gained a third view: Errors (`v` now cycles Users → Events → Errors). It scans the same access-log window for 5xx responses and ranks the paths producing them, with the per-status breakdown (500×3, 502×1, …), how many distinct IPs hit each failure and when it last happened, plus the overall 5xx share of all requests in the summary — so a path that quietly started erroring stands out without grepping the logs. A window with traffic but no 5xx at all says so explicitly instead of showing an empty table. Admin paths are excluded, same as everywhere else in the traffic analysis.
- Rows in the Traffic screen's Events view are now selectable, like IPs in the Users view already were: enter opens the event's detail — venue and city, status, genre, submitter, and its full day-by-day view history (hits and visitors per day, newest first) inside the trend window. For view counts whose event has since been deleted, the detail says so and still shows the surviving daily numbers.

The card previously always ranked the whole view history, so long-finished shows crowded out everything still to come; it now lists only upcoming events by default and shows the full history when the switch is on (`/admin/statistics?past=1`). The filter runs in SQL rather than hiding rows in the markup, so the upcoming view is still a full top 25 instead of whatever survived the cut, and deleted events only appear in the past view, since without a date there is nothing to rank them against.

- Added inbound scraping detection: a `before_request` hook (`diytracker/services/scrape_detection.py`) scores anonymous traffic per IP over a 10-minute sliding window against signals that also catch common evasion tricks — a honeypot page linked invisibly in the footer and disallowed in `robots.txt` (which now exists, served by Flask, honeypot and admin paths disallowed, sitemap referenced); known HTTP-library/headless user agents and missing UAs; *spoofed* browser UAs (claims a browser but sends no `Accept-Language`, or claims modern Chrome without `sec-ch-ua` client hints); sustained request volume; metronomic inter-request timing that survives randomised sleep jitter; sequential `/events/<id>` enumeration; deep crawls with no same-origin navigation; and the same header fingerprint arriving from many IPs at once (rotating proxies — only counted alongside another signal, since stock browsers share fingerprints). IPs crossing the threshold are persisted to a new `scrape_suspect` table (auto-created by `db.create_all()`, writes throttled to once a minute per IP) and listed with their score, signal badges, UA and sample paths on a new admin page at `/admin/scrape-suspects` ("Suspects" in the admin nav), with a clear-list button. Detection only — nothing is blocked. State is in-memory per gunicorn worker (same trade-off as flask-limiter), so volume thresholds are effectively up to 4× with 4 workers.
- Made the scraper a much politer citizen, cutting its load on petzi.ch by ~99%. The prod log showed ~6,300 requests/day re-fetching the same ~342 petzi pages every hour: rows rejected as non-concerts (or invalid) were never recorded, so every run re-downloaded them. Rejected URLs now land in a new `skipped_url` table (auto-created by `db.create_all()`) that feeds the known-URL dedup set. Also: sitemap-index scraping now takes hints to skip irrelevant sub-sitemaps (metalgigs' 2.1 MB bands and 180 KB venues sitemaps are no longer downloaded hourly), the scrape interval went from 1 to 6 hours, the scraper honors each host's robots.txt (parsers cached per host for a day), identifies itself with an honest `diytracker (+https://diytracker.ch; …)` User-Agent instead of rotating fake browser strings, and no longer retries on 403 (a "go away" isn't a transient error; 429 with Retry-After is still retried).

The map card also drops its 78vh height on small screens for a 10:7 aspect ratio matching Switzerland's shape, removing the empty paper bands above and below the country.

The restart no longer needs the root password: a new sudoers drop-in (`deploy/sudoers-diytracker`, installed once to `/etc/sudoers.d/`) lets `diytrackeruser` run exactly `systemctl restart diytracker.service` passwordless.
